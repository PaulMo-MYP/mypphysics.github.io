Where the analogy breaks

  1. Everything stops at once. Turn the light red and every car in the city stops together, not just the ones near the light. That is what a circuit does: the loop is already full of charge, so a break anywhere stops the drift everywhere almost instantly. Real traffic would queue behind the light while the far side of town emptied.
  2. Real charge doesn't bunch up. Here the cars crowd together and slow down in the narrow streets. In a metal the carrier density n is fixed by the material, so from I = nAvq a thinner conductor carrying the same current has a higher drift speed, not a lower one. A resistor's real effect is to reduce the current all the way round the loop, and the simulation does show that.
  3. The speeds are a mapping, not a measurement. 1 A is shown as 35 km/h on a highway and 12 km/h on a narrow street. Real drift speeds are around a tenth of a millimetre per second. A lamp lights at once because the electric field is set up round the circuit at close to the speed of light, not because the charges race round.
  4. The cars follow conventional current, leaving the + end of the depot. In a metal the electrons drift the other way.
  5. Energy, not cars, gets used up. Cars are never lost. Each one gains energy at the depot (the emf) and spends it on the narrow streets (the pd across each resistor), where it becomes heat. Because the highways are ideal wires, crossing them costs nothing.
